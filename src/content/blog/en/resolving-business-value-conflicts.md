---
title: "Who Decides When Business Values Conflict?"
description: "Even when business value guides decisions, different values can conflict. I consider who should take responsibility for conflicts that affect the overall direction while delegating decisions within individual areas."

In the previous article, I concluded that both business processes and technology are means of delivering the business value we want to provide.
This time, I want to ask who decides when there are several such values and they conflict.

For example, convenience and safety, immediacy and fairness, and short-term results and long-term sustainability can all be business values.
Even if we say we should choose whichever offers more value to users, can't those values themselves compete?

## Delegate individual decisions to teams with focused responsibilities

Centralizing every decision does not necessarily produce the best overall outcome.
Those at the center cannot understand every detail of every area, and requiring approval even for individual decisions would likely make decision-making itself a bottleneck.

It seems better for each team to have a narrow, clearly defined scope of responsibility and make the necessary decisions within it.
What should be narrow here is the scope of responsibility, not the team's authority.
Delegating decisions within an area to those closest to it seems natural.

## Someone needs to take responsibility for conflicts that affect the whole

Yet even when each area makes reasonable decisions, values may still conflict across the whole product.
Leaving those decisions distributed across the individual areas will not resolve the conflict.

For now, I think it makes sense to have one person, such as a PO (product owner), responsible for value judgments across the product, and to escalate only the conflicts that individual areas cannot resolve.
This person would not decide everything, but would bear final responsibility for trade-offs affecting the overall direction.

## There can be no universal evaluation criteria

What, then, should that person base their decisions on?
I do not think we can establish universal evaluation criteria or an objective function here.

Priorities change depending on the users, the stage of the business, the time horizon, the risks, and the constraints.
Business value itself also evolves as we act.
Perhaps what needs to remain fixed is not what takes priority, but the structure of responsibility: who makes which decisions, and who ultimately takes responsibility when they conflict.

Decisions within individual areas are delegated, while a clearly designated person decides on conflicts between values that affect the overall direction, taking the circumstances at the time into account.
For now, this seems a practical way to aim for the best overall outcome.
