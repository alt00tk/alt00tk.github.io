Since I began letting AI handle implementation, I have been writing less code.
Instead, I have more time for design.
Even if my intuition for implementation weakens, could my intuition for design actually grow?

## Is More Experience Enough?

With AI, I can quickly try ideas that used to take time to implement.
I form a hypothesis myself, explore possible approaches with AI, and check them through testing and by examining the results of changes.
If I can repeat this cycle faster, I should be able to gain more experience in the same amount of time.

Still, accepting 100 AI suggestions as they are is different from going through 10 cases in which I compare options, choose an approach, and reflect on the outcome myself.
If I work through 100 cases and understand clear, well-founded explanations for them, quantity matters too. But can simply receiving answers count as experience in making judgments?

Repeating similar problems seems likely to help consolidate learning.
But experiencing varied cases, including conditions that reverse a judgment, may be what reveals the limits of a principle's applicability.

## Are Understanding and Articulation the Same?

To develop intuition, do I need to articulate the patterns myself every time?

[Reber's (1967) experiment on implicit learning](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-5371(67)80149-X) reported that people could recognize grammatical regularities in unfamiliar stimuli without being explicitly taught the rules of an artificial grammar.
However, this finding, as described in the publicly available abstract, cannot simply be generalized to the development of intuition for complex software design.

If someone can predict where problems might arise from an unfamiliar change, even without being able to explain why it is risky, I would like to think that some form of understanding is present.
Articulation is not understanding itself; it also helps us check our understanding and communicate it to others.
It should also be possible to learn to articulate something later.

## How Can I Check My Intuition?

Even so, I do not change a design on intuition alone.
I use a sense that something is off as a starting point for investigation. If verification reveals no evidence supporting that concern, I make a decision based on the evidence currently available.
I keep that unease as an unresolved hypothesis.

[Kahneman and Klein (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0016755) argue that evaluating the reliability of intuition requires attention to the predictability of the environment and opportunities to learn its regularities, and that subjective confidence is not a reliable indicator of accuracy (based on the publicly available abstract).
There also seems to be value in recording my predictions and comparing them with actual outcomes.

## What Should I Delegate to AI?

I can delegate the gathering of cases and their organization for comparison to AI.
But if I also delegate the discovery of common criteria for judgment across those cases, I may lose opportunities to learn.
For my part, I want to go through as much of that process myself as possible.

For now, I see AI as something that changes the focus of intuition and the experiences through which it develops, rather than simply weakening it.
But I still do not know how much of that experience I should retain by continuing to discover patterns for myself.
